Chapter 1: The first inquest. By the time the palace bells struck the hour they later denied striking, the Hall of Petitions had already been arranged as a theater of grief. The prince lay under a linen cloth, or the prince's double did, or no body lay there at all. The official narrator remembers a wrist, a signet, and a drop of black cordial on the marble. The same narrator, after consultation with the Blue Censor, remembers only a cloth and the odor of extinguished candles. Both versions are entered here because the palace preferred contradictions to gaps.

Attention, loyal residents and temporary witnesses: the Redacted Palace is under ceremonious lockdown. No one may leave by gate, gallery, laundry flue, chapel drain, mirror service, or diplomatic exception until the inquest into His Highness's inconvenience is complete. Please return borrowed mourning gloves to the silver trays. Please refrain from naming any corpse before the Office of Names issues a receipt. Any person hearing a voice from a wall, pipe, stair, portrait, or sealed dumbwaiter should answer only with rank, not memory.

"I, Aria Venn, palace historian, began this account in black ink and good faith," Aria said, though the first phrase had already been crossed out twice. "I revise only what evidence compels me to revise. If the first witness called the dead man Prince Cael, and the second witness called him the Lantern Guest, and the third begged me to write no title at all, then my duty is not certainty. My duty is to preserve the shape of the lie until truth can be measured against it. Strike that. My duty is obedience with footnotes."

"When a guest drops his napkin before the fish course, the correct servant lifts it with the left hand," Malek Dorn told the junior keymen, his voice as mild as a primer on forks. "If the guest drops a body before the fifth toast, the correct servant closes the east locks first, the south locks second, and the west locks never. Anyone who forgets that order has displayed unforgivable rudeness toward the crown." He bowed toward the inquest table so deeply that only Vesper noticed the folded order sliding from his cuff into the judge's empty cup.

"I tasted the cordial," Tarek Moul said. "It contained cherry, bitter almond, ceremonial pepper, and a second bitterness I was asked not to name because the name would make several people look at the wrong door. The cup touched the prince's lips, unless the lips belonged to the man called prince for seating purposes. I swallowed one spoonful and did not die. That does not prove safety. It proves only that the dose did not choose me, or that my body has poor manners toward murder."

"The record will show that Officer Vesper Linn removed inflammatory matter," Vesper said, holding her blue pencil above the page as if it were a surgical knife. "Inflammatory matter includes panic, treason, excessive metaphor, and any title not recognized by the current hour. The phrase 'prince's double' is provisionally [REDACTED]. The phrase 'living corpse' is aesthetically vulgar and administratively unsound. The phrase 'Blue Censor' may remain because nicknames disarm the frightened. I prefer them frightened but not imaginative."

Selene Io smiled from the witness chair and spoke first in her own bright register. Then, without moving her shoulders, she continued in Malek Dorn's measured baritone: "When a guest drops his napkin, lock the east door." She returned to herself before the clerk could gasp. "That is mimicry, not confession. I sing what I hear. Last night I heard Captain Dorn give etiquette lessons to a hinge. I also heard the prince, or a prince-shaped man, humming my lullaby in a voice that belonged to no throat I have ever kissed, taught, or feared."

"For the minutes between the glass course and the last toast, I was addressed as His Highness by persons paid to know better," Nolan Crest said. "At dawn I was called the Prince's Mercy, at breakfast the Empty Prince, and in Vesper's private index the Spare Face. I do not object to titles when they come with appropriate stipends. I object when they are used to bury a living man under another man's applause." He touched the signet on his left hand, then hid the hand as if it had contradicted him.

"The body under the linen was warm when I examined it," Rhea Sarn said. "The official certificate says cold because the official certificate was prepared before I was admitted. I found no royal birthmark, no wine stain on the tongue, and no stiffness except the kind produced by servants waiting to be blamed. If you want medicine to flatter politics, send for a court sculptor. If you want medicine to speak, let the certificate bleed a little." She placed a clean scalpel beside the sealed report and watched Vesper's pencil hover.

"I prune the orange trees above the old ventilation court," Omar Jett said, twisting soil from the cuff of his sleeve. "At night the vents breathe upward. That means the palace talks to gardeners whether it intends to or not. I heard a woman's voice saying, 'Wake the double before the song ends.' Then a man's voice answered, 'No, wake the title.' Then Selene's voice laughed from a pipe that had no room for Selene. If a wall repeats a singer, does the witness blame the singer, the wall, or the person who taught stone to gossip?"

"Let the inquest accept four premises," Livia Quen said, arranging almonds into a proof on the table. "First, only nine keys can reach the apricot gallery. Second, Malek controlled seven, Vesper sealed one, and the missing key appears in no honest pocket. Third, Tarek lived after tasting the cordial. Fourth, Nolan was seen in two rooms whose walking distance exceeds the time between sightings unless one Nolan was not Nolan. Therefore at least one statement here is a performance. I withhold which one until the palace stops cheating at arithmetic."

Margin note, hand unknown: The historian's first draft named the dead man Cael Veyr, but the Blue Censor scraped the letters thin enough to see daylight through them. Under the erasure is another title, the Noon Crown, which no herald has used aloud since the west nursery burned. Intercepted whisper copied from vent seven: "If the double speaks before the ambassador counts, Vesper wins." The note is unsigned because signatures have a way of becoming bodies in this house.

The inquest table had twelve chairs for eleven authorities and one absence. Aria placed herself at the end reserved for historians, which was also the end reserved for convenient blame. Malek stood by the key rack. Selene sat where acoustics favored her. Tarek sat near water. Rhea refused the stool offered to medical staff and took a chair from a duke. Omar remained damp with garden night though the morning had advanced. Livia watched everyone like an equation that had started lying about its variables.

Chapter 2: The banquet reconstructed, then damaged. At the Glass Banquet, every servant wore a mask etched with a guest's family crest, and every guest wore a mask etched with a servant's number. The narrator would like to claim this confused the murderer, but the palace has always used confusion as a form of architecture. Fifteen minutes before the prince drank, the chandeliers dimmed. Ten minutes before he drank, the intercom sang a menu. Five minutes before he drank, someone redacted a door from the map. None of this proves design, which is exactly how design survives.

Course five is served: glass pheasant, apricot salt, memory pears, and the ceremonial cordial. Guests seated under black ribbons should drink only after the crowned hand rises. Guests seated under blue ribbons should observe silence unless silence has been requisitioned. The Redacted Palace reminds all diners that imitation of royal speech is punishable by demotion, except during licensed musical entertainments, authorized emergency substitutions, or ceremonies already marked [REDACTED]. Please enjoy the appearance of continuity.

"I was licensed musical entertainment until the second chandelier failed," Selene said. "After that I became a convenient rumor with cheekbones. I sang the old hunting round because the prince requested it. Then Nolan, wearing the prince's left-handed ring on his right hand, requested the same song in the prince's voice. Then someone behind the screen requested silence in my voice. If you ask whose command I obeyed, I answer: the loudest purse, then the sharpest fear, then art, in that humiliating order."

"I was not behind the screen," Nolan said. "I was in front of three hundred witnesses, wearing forty pounds of brocade and the smile of a man hoping no one asks him a childhood question. The real prince, if that title still points to a person, hated pears and adored ceremonies where others swallowed first. I was told to raise the cup, not drink it. The man under the linen drank because someone called him by my secret title: Ash Cousin. Only two people knew it, and one of them was supposed to be dead."

"The banquet ledger places Nolan at seat one and Prince Cael at seat none," Aria said, then stopped to amend herself. "No, the ledger places His Highness at seat one and a blank at seat none. No, the ledger was rewritten after midnight by a clerk whose hand trembled toward Vesper's office. I have three ledgers, all official, all incompatible. This is not unusual. A palace makes history by issuing several pasts and rewarding the citizen who chooses the safest."

"A courteous server approaches the royal left only if the right side is obstructed," Malek said to the panel, which had not asked about serving positions. "If the right side is obstructed by a singer, a physician, or an ambassador making a tower of almonds, the server apologizes to the nearest captain of keys. The captain may then authorize a route through the chapel corridor. The chapel corridor, due to sacred inconvenience, was unrecorded for seven minutes. I regret the lapse in etiquette." He did not regret it visibly.

"Captain Dorn's lapse will be described as procedural mist," Vesper said. "Mist is not evidence. Mist implies climate, and climate cannot be hanged. The chapel corridor was closed, opened, closed again, and finally removed from vocabulary at my instruction. Before anyone performs outrage, remember that an unredacted map during succession panic is an invitation to civil choreography. I save lives by making certain paths impossible to discuss. If some witnesses vanish into those paths, that is a separate department's poetry."

"The body had apricot salt beneath the fingernails," Rhea said. "Not because he ate with his hands. Because he scratched the underside of the table where the salt was hidden in a crack. A poisoned man reaches for his throat, his cup, or a god, depending on education. This man reached under the table. That is not panic. That is retrieval. I also found wax on his cuff, blue wax, which means the order that killed him may have been sealed before dinner and opened during dessert."

"I did not taste wax," Tarek said. "Wax is not assigned to tasters unless someone has lost patience with grammar. I tasted the salt after Doctor Sarn pointed at it. It contained apricot, ash, and a preservative used in embalming rooms. I was told not to call it embalming salt because guests dislike being seasoned with implications. I therefore call it table salt that expected a corpse. That is accurate enough for my tongue and too accurate for several faces here."

"The chapel corridor has a vent behind the saint with removable eyes," Omar said. "Gardeners know because the saint leaks when we overwater the west beds. During course five I heard shoes there. Soft court shoes, military boots, and one person barefoot, which is how singers walk when they are saving noise for later. I am not saying Selene was there. I am saying a barefoot person hummed exactly as Selene warms up, except the hum ended in a cough she does not own."

"A corridor unrecorded for seven minutes can still be bounded by consequences," Livia said. "Let C be the corridor, S the salt, W the wax, and K the missing key. If C contains no witness, then S travels by servant. If S travels by servant, Malek knows the route. If Malek knows the route and Vesper erases it, their intersection is not guilt, but opportunity. Opportunity plus motive is not proof. Opportunity plus a dead substitute plus Nolan's hidden title begins to resemble a theorem with blood on the variables."

One is mechanically perfect and therefore inhuman, likely palace pipe resonance. Intercepted whisper, source grille damaged: "Do not poison the prince. Poison the witness who can be counted as prince." Beneath that, another hand adds: Or make counting impossible.

Chapter 3: Keys, doors, and other polite weapons. The palace contained nine master keys, though this fact was classified because monarchies dislike arithmetic when it belongs to locksmiths. Malek wore seven on a ceremonial chain. The eighth slept in Vesper's blue cabinet. The ninth had been buried with the previous captain, except the previous captain's coffin had no bones, only brass filings and a folded invitation to the current banquet. This is not foreshadowing, the narrator insists, and the narrator has been wrong more efficiently than most people are honest.

"A locked door is a guest who has not been properly introduced," Malek said, lifting one key at a time. "The apricot gallery met me at dusk, the chapel corridor at seven bells, the north crypt never. Anyone claiming I opened the north crypt should also claim I served soup with a sword. It would be equally vulgar." He glanced at Nolan, then added, "When a false prince requests a true room, the captain may delay him by discussing table linens. Delay is not refusal. It is etiquette wearing armor."

"Captain Dorn delayed me because he thought I was false in the wrong direction," Nolan said. "He had orders to protect the prince from the double and the double from the prince. Imagine being a mirror with guards posted on both sides. I asked for the north crypt because the real Cael left me a note there. It said, 'Little Ash, if I die publicly, look for me privately.' That line is melodrama, yes. Cael was raised by tutors who mistook chandeliers for character."

"I can confirm an old ledger mentions Little Ash," Aria said. "I cannot confirm whether it names Nolan, Cael, or the boy with burned hands removed from the nursery rolls. I wrote that removal when I was nineteen. I wrote it because my superior said the child had died, and because the child was standing behind him, very much alive, wearing a hood. Strike this from the official reading. No, leave it. If I am to be useful, let me at least be precisely ashamed."

"Historian Venn is confusing personal memory with admissible record," Vesper said. "The nursery incident is sealed under mercy statutes. The name Little Ash is sealed under succession statutes. The relation between mercy and succession is sealed under statutes I am not permitted to name because naming them would violate the statute naming statute. You may laugh. The palace survives because absurdity exhausts curiosity. By the time you finish mocking the form, the fact has escaped through the servant stair."

"The burned child survived," Rhea said. "I know because I reset two fingers on a boy who would not tell me his name but answered when a nurse said Nolan. Years later I treated Prince Cael for the same scar pattern, except his were painted for a festival. Bodies carry records no censor can blue over. They also carry lies. A scar can be copied, softened, or invoked by a good actor. What cannot be copied is old pain when rain moves in. Nolan's hand hurt before the storm began."

"I tasted the key chain once," Tarek said, when the panel asked whether he had inspected it. "Not by assignment. I fell during a rehearsal and my mouth struck brass. The seventh key had salt in its grooves. The ninth key, if that was the ninth key on Captain Dorn's chain last night, tasted of soil, coffin cloth, and garden lime. That means either the buried key came back, or someone taught a newer key to remember a grave. I dislike both flavors."

"Garden lime went missing from my shed three nights before the banquet," Omar said. "I reported it to the laundry because the laundry reports everything faster than guards. The next day Malek sent me a note about pruning dead branches near formal windows. In gardener language that means prune branches. In palace language, I think it meant stop asking about lime. The note was beautiful, almost tender. He threatened me with punctuation."

Selene leaned forward and used Omar's rough cadence so perfectly that even Omar flinched: "He threatened me with punctuation." Then she returned to herself. "Forgive me. Mimicry is my way of holding evidence before it evaporates. The note Omar received was read aloud in a corridor by someone imitating Malek badly. The vowels were right, the mercy was wrong. Captain Dorn sounds like a knife wrapped in a napkin. The corridor voice sounded like a napkin dreaming of teeth."

"We now have two key hypotheses," Livia said. "Either Malek possessed the ninth key and used etiquette as camouflage, or an imitator possessed a key flavored like burial and used Malek as camouflage. The probability of each changes when Selene speaks because she increases the set of possible voices without increasing the set of bodies. This is why singers terrify courts. A theorem wants each symbol to mean one thing. A singer can make one throat carry a parliament."

Reminder: all keys are property of the crown, even when temporarily attached to persons, graves, cabinets, ghosts, or metaphors. Unauthorized tasting of keys is discouraged. Unauthorized discussion of nursery ash is prohibited under the Mercy of Silence. Guests experiencing uncertainty about whether they are themselves or an officially assigned replacement should report to the Mirror Office between the hours of never and immediately. Do not attempt to leave through doors that remember you differently.

The palace used jokes as safes because no one searches a laugh for hinges. Intercepted whisper from service stair: "If Malek bows twice, give the key to the woman with blue hands." Chapter 4: The Blue Censor opens a door by erasing it. Vesper Linn's office stood behind a wall of portraits whose painted eyes had been scratched away. This had not been done by vandals. It was a professional courtesy. Portraits that cannot look cannot be subpoenaed. The inquest entered after Malek performed an apology at the brass plate and the plate, embarrassed by etiquette, became a handle. Inside were drawers marked with colors rather than names. Blue meant living secrets. Black meant dead secrets. White meant secrets not yet invented but already funded.

"Do not mistake my office for a tomb," Vesper said. "Tombs are honest. They admit what they store. My office is an emergency lung for the palace. When the court inhales scandal, I exhale order. Yes, I redacted the prince's breakfast tutors, the nursery fire, and the guest who left through a painting. I did not redact the poisoning. I redacted the word poisoning because it gives murder too much confidence. Until a motive survives examination, the official term is adverse ceremony."

"Adverse ceremony appears in thirteen earlier reigns," Aria said, pulling open a drawer before Vesper could stop her. "In nine cases it meant poisoning. In two cases it meant a duel disguised as a hymn. In one it meant a queen simply walked out and dared the palace to call absence treason. In the last, every line is blue. I wrote the index entry, but the handwriting beside it is mine from a year I do not remember. I request permission to be alarmed by myself."

"Memory gaps are symptoms, not sins," Rhea said. "Blue ink contains powdered sleep-root if prepared by the old office. Clerks who lick their pencils while revising may lose hours, days, or inconvenient loyalties. Historian Venn's tongue has a blue crescent on the left side. Vesper's does not. That means Vesper either never licks the pencil, or has arranged to make others swallow the work. I am giving a medical observation, not an accusation. The palace has made that distinction difficult on purpose."

"I tasted a blue pencil when I was promoted," Tarek said. "It produced numbness, obedience, and a dream in which my mother asked me to inventory the moon. I reported this. The report returned with my signature and a sentence saying I enjoyed the moon. I did not sign that sentence. I did enjoy my mother, but not as evidence. I would like that distinction recorded before someone improves it."

"A good guest never points at a hostess's medicine cabinet," Malek said. "A good captain, however, notices when the cabinet contains keys instead of medicine and medicine instead of orders. Officer Linn, by the custom of the west supper, may I compliment the lock on your white drawer? It is too new for an old office and too scratched for a new crime." His compliment meant open it now. Every keyman in the room understood. Vesper smiled because she had understood first.

Vesper opened the white drawer and showed them a card with all words removed except Nolan Crest, No, and crown. "This is why censorship is kinder than witness speech," she said. "A witness leaves context lying around like furniture in a dark room. Redaction clears the floor. Nolan Crest no crown. Nolan Crest no corpse. Nolan Crest no exit. You see? Three facts, no injury." Her face remained composed until Selene repeated the sentence in a child's voice from the far corner.

"Nolan Crest no crown," Selene sang in the voice of a child who had learned fear before grammar. "Nolan Crest no corpse. Nolan Crest no exit." The room chilled around the tune. Selene pressed two fingers to her throat and frowned. "That was not mine. I know how I steal voices. That one arrived as if the wall lent it to me. There is a nursery behind this office, or there was, or the office has been built around a memory with better acoustics than mercy."

"I remember that rhyme," Nolan said. "Do not ask whether I heard it as myself or as Cael. In the nursery, they called one of us Little Ash and one of us Little Crown. Some days they changed which was which. A tutor said identity should be flexible in service of the realm. Then the curtains caught fire, and flexibility became smoke. If Vesper has that rhyme in a drawer, she has more of my childhood than I do. I would like it returned, preferably unarmed."

"Behind Vesper's portraits is a wet wall," Omar said. "Roots found it years ago. I cut them twice, and twice they came back blue at the tips. Plants do not read statutes. They drink whatever truth leaks through plaster. The orange tree nearest this office grows bitter fruit on one side and sweet fruit on the other. I thought it was soil. Now I think half the tree is listening to a nursery and half to a censor. That makes bad marmalade and worse history."

"If an office contains a nursery memory, a redaction drug, a future order, and a missing title, then its function is not concealment alone," Livia said. "It is transformation. Input: child. Output: prince, double, witness, or absence, according to need. The palace is not asking who killed the prince. It is asking which variable may be assigned the value prince after the killing. That is a different problem, and more dangerous because it has many correct answers."

Unattributed correction in blue dust: Vesper did not create the office. Vesper inherited it from three women, two men, and one person known only as the Quiet Plural. The Blue Censor is a job, a title, and possibly a plural role concealed in a single face. Intercepted whisper, nursery wall: "She is not erasing us. She is keeping us from being used twice." A second hand answers: Or saving herself first.

Chapter 5: Songs in borrowed throats. The palace chapel had been designed for worship, coronations, emergency trials, and operas commissioned by rulers who wanted piety to applaud. Selene stood beneath the cracked rose window while the inquest gathered below. The narrator will admit, under protest, that she was beautiful in the way a blade is beautiful after it has learned restraint. This detail is irrelevant except that irrelevant details often smuggle motive past guards who inspect only facts.

"I will sing the banquet as heard," Selene said. "Not as seen. Sight lies when masks are expensive." She began with Tarek's careful syllables: "Cherry, almond, pepper." She shifted to Nolan's court brightness: "Raise, not drink." She became Malek: "East lock first." She became Vesper: "Adverse ceremony." Then, with effort visible in her jaw, she became a voice no one claimed: "Little Crown drinks after Little Ash falls." She stopped there because Rhea had risen and because Nolan had gone colorless enough to resemble his title.

"Stop," Rhea said. "That last voice produced a trauma response in two witnesses and a memory seizure in one historian. I am not using poetry. Aria's pulse dropped when the phrase Little Crown began. Nolan's scar hand clenched before the word falls. Vesper's pupils narrowed at drinks. Bodies answer before rehearsed loyalty can prepare a statement. Continue if you wish to solve the case by damaging every witness. I prefer slower cruelty with better notes."

"I have heard that voice," Aria said, gripping the chapel rail. "I wrote a nursery inventory while it dictated. It belonged to no one in the room and everyone who had authority over children. I remember a masked governor, a priest, and the old Blue Censor speaking in turns, but the same sentence came out of each mouth. 'A crown is a task, not a boy.' I crossed out boy and wrote instrument. That correction has followed me for half my life."

"When Selene used that voice, I remembered burning curtains and Cael laughing because he thought the sparks were stars," Nolan said. "Then I remembered being Cael watching Nolan cry. Then I remembered a tutor telling us the kingdom needed one prince and one spare witness. Memory should not have camera angles. Mine has at least three. If you are wondering whether that makes me unreliable, congratulations. The palace built me to be unreliable and then asked me to swear on consistency."

"In matters of song, a guest offers applause only after the final note," Malek said. "In matters of danger, a captain may interrupt with a dropped glove." He dropped one white glove at Selene's feet, then a black one near Nolan. To most observers it looked theatrical. To his keymen it meant isolate the singer and guard the double. To Vesper it meant Malek had stopped hiding orders inside etiquette and begun hiding rebellion inside etiquette, which was not the same flavor at all.

"Captain Dorn, retrieve your accessories," Vesper said. "This is an inquest, not a masked drill." She picked up the black glove with the tips of two fingers. A slip of paper fell from it: If she sings the third child, break the north glass. Vesper read it once, folded it, and placed it in her sleeve instead of the record. "The glove contained lint," she announced. "No more." Her lie was small, crisp, and expensive.

"The black glove tasted of cedar, oil, and fear," Tarek said, because he had retrieved it when no one watched Vesper's sleeve closely enough. "It also tasted of palace glass dust. I know that dust. Windows in the north nursery use sand from the black river, not the south quarries. If a person wished to break north glass, they would make a sound like winter plates falling. During the banquet, when the prince fell, I heard winter plates, though no one served plates until the next course."

"The phrase third child matters," Livia said. "We have been modeling two: prince and double, Little Crown and Little Ash. Introduce a third, and every binary contradiction becomes underdetermined. A double may not be a spare of the prince but a shield for another heir, or a witness trained to remember whatever the heir must forget. If Selene sings the third child, Malek expects glass to break. Therefore the third child's name is stored in resonance, not paper. That is both clever and barbaric."

"The chapel rose window has vines behind it," Omar said. "Not painted vines. Real ones, thin as hair. They run from the old nursery wall to the organ pipes. I thought them dead until Selene sang, and then every vine tightened like it was listening. If you want the third child's name, ask the plants. They have been fed by rain, plaster, and secrets. They may be kinder than us because no one taught them rank."

Chapter 6: The physician contradicts the dead. Rhea Sarn had been summoned to confirm a royal death and had instead become offensively interested in the body. The body, for its part, continued to be unhelpful. It possessed a face convenient to Nolan's testimony, hands inconvenient to Prince Cael's portraits, and a throat whose bruises suggested either poison, strangulation, or a collar worn by someone who expected to be dragged backward through a narrow panel. The narrator recommends poison because poison is tidy. Rhea refused tidiness as if it were malpractice.

"There are three injuries," Rhea said. "First, a burned tongue from cordial too hot to serve, which means the drink was heated after tasting or Tarek was given a decoy. Second, bruising under the jaw from a hand smaller than Malek's and stronger than Aria's. Third, a needle mark behind the left ear containing blue sleep-root, not enough to kill, enough to soften memory. The dead man was managed before he was murdered. If that distinction bores the court, the court is welcome to die less administratively."

"I was given a decoy," Tarek said. "The cup I tasted had a chip near the rim. The prince's cup had no chip when raised. After the fall, the cup on the floor had a chip and my tooth mark beside it. That means someone exchanged cups twice: once after I tasted, once after he drank. I do not understand why anyone would do this unless the exchange was the message. I taste objects, not motives. Motives leave fewer minerals."

"A well-run table never requires the same cup to be in two places," Malek said. "If a cup travels, a server accompanies it; if a server travels, a key permits passage; if a key permits passage, a captain knows whether permission was requested politely. No such request came to me. Therefore either the cup moved rudely, which is improbable, or the cup did not move and the witnesses moved around a lie shaped like porcelain. Etiquette cannot solve murder, but it can identify bad stagecraft."

"I can stage a cup moving without moving it," Selene said. She tapped the table twice and threw her voice behind the judge. A clerk turned toward the sound of breaking porcelain though nothing had broken. "Sound is a servant that does not need a corridor. During the banquet, I heard the chip before I saw it. Someone wanted witnesses to look down, then up, then at the wrong hand. I did not make that sound. I know because I was busy being paid to sound like a nightingale, which is less useful and better dressed."

"The hand that raised the cup was mine," Nolan said. "The hand that dropped it may also have been mine, but I was not inside it at the time. Laugh if you want. There are rooms in this palace where a person can be addressed so intensely by a title that the body obeys before the mind votes. Last night someone said, 'Noon Crown, drink.' I am not the Noon Crown. My hand disagreed. That should interest anyone who prefers crimes with fewer ghosts."

"The phrase possession will be removed," Vesper said. "The phrase title-conditioned response may remain if Doctor Sarn promises not to enjoy it. We have long known that court training produces reflex. Bow at bells, kneel at anthem, smile at treason until the guards understand which side has won. Nolan was trained as a double. Doubles are responsive instruments. If someone played the correct title, that does not make the palace haunted. It makes our pedagogy embarrassingly effective."

"Title-conditioned response appears in the old nursery manuals," Aria said. "I cataloged them under ceremonial education because the alternative category, weaponized identity, lacked royal approval. The manual lists Little Crown, Little Ash, and a blank third column cut from every surviving copy. I can see the cuts. Whoever removed the name used a round blade and patience. Historians are trained to read what remains. The remains say three children were taught to answer to three functions: rule, absorb, remember."

"Then the body problem changes again," Livia said. "If one child rules, one absorbs danger, and one remembers, a murder at the banquet may target the remembering function, not the ruling function. A dead rememberer would free the palace to revise every prior crime. Conversely, a living rememberer could make the palace vulnerable to proof. We should identify whether the dead man was prince, double, or archive. The categories are monstrous, but monstrous categories can still be exhaustive."

"The body smelled of rue from the old herb court," Omar said. "We stopped growing rue five years ago after it made the bees vicious. Someone kept a pot alive under glass. I saw it in the physician's annex once, and Rhea yelled at me for touching the shade. I am not accusing her. I am saying the palace accuses through inventory. If rue appears on a corpse, every person who knows where rue survives becomes a suspect, including the gardener who noticed too much."

"I kept rue because sleep-root antidotes need it," Rhea said. "The official pharmacy destroyed its stock after Vesper classified induced forgetting as nonmedical.
